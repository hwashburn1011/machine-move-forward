# Machine Move Forward — Roadmap to 1.0

> **Status:** Approved direction, 2026-08-26.
> **What this is:** The high-level roadmap from the current build to a releasable
> 1.0. Each phase below gets its own brainstorm → design spec → implementation
> plan before any code is written; this document decides order and intent, not
> implementation.
> **Relationship to the handoff:** This supersedes the milestone list in
> `machine-move-forward-game-handoff.md` (§47). The handoff remains the
> reference for system-level detail; where the two disagree, this document wins.

---

## 1. Identity

A PvE survival shooter-looter on a continuously moving walking machine crossing
a hostile desert.

- **From Raft:** the chill loop — building, crafting, gentle survival needs,
  making the vehicle a home — and a story drip-fed through signals that lead to
  hand-authored destinations.
- **From SAND:** the desert-machine world, gun gameplay, and machine-scale
  combat — crewed enemy vehicles that pull alongside, shoot, and board.
- **The distinctive spine:** the machine is simultaneously home, vehicle,
  fortress, factory, and progression object, and the player's own construction
  is the combat arena.

Decisions fixed during roadmap brainstorming (2026-08-26):

- **Story shape:** Raft-style. Radio signals give leads; hand-authored
  landmarks appear on or near the route, each holding journals, environmental
  storytelling, a unique reward, and the lead to the next. Navigation unlocks
  are story rewards.
- **Combat arc:** PvE only. Bots boarding (today) → crewed enemy vehicles that
  stop alongside, shoot at the machine, and board it → progression gives the
  player real control and maneuvering of their own machine. No PvP; no player
  boarding of enemy machines in 1.0.
- **Survival:** a light layer. Water and food as forgiving pressures that make
  calm phases purposeful and the machine feel like a home. Never punishing.
- **End state:** a complete, releasable small game — roughly a three-act story
  arc ending at a final destination, ~8–15 hours, browser-based — built so it
  can be expanded with new chapters afterward, the way Raft was.

## 2. Premise (placeholder — fixed for real in Phase 9)

You are the last crew member of a walking machine locked on an old caravan
route across a lethal desert. A faint recurring radio signal promises somewhere
worth going. The game is the journey: keep the machine alive, make it home,
fight off what the desert sends, and follow the signal — earning, piece by
piece, the ability to choose your own course.

**The opening (placeholder, expanded later):** the game begins not on the
machine but above it — the player chased across a ruined rooftop at the
desert's edge by scavenger robots, backed onto a ledge with nowhere left to
run, as the machine walks past below. The story starts with a leap of faith
onto its deck, and the machine carries the player out into the dunes.

## 3. Where the build stands (2026-08-26)

**Done:** foundation and procedural art pipeline; the walker (articulated legs,
gait, deck-carrying); third-person shooter core with rifle and shotgun; grid
build system with rooms; inventory, storage, and crafting; the salvage reel;
the threat director; two infantry enemies that path over player-built
structures; synthesized audio; versioned saves. (Handoff milestones 0–5 and 10.)

**In flight:** machine damage & repair
(`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-26-machine-damage-repair.md`).

**Known gaps that gate later phases:** fuel is storable but inert (no power
system); enclosed interiors are dark (no lamps); the stairs fix is measured
against the running game but not yet proven by a scripted harness walk.

## 4. The shape of the roadmap

Five acts. Each act ends with a visibly better "20-minute session" and
alternates the chill loop and the combat loop so neither starves. Story enters
in Act III, once there is a world worth narrating.

Standing rule inherited from the handoff and kept: **every phase ships
playable**, with unit tests for deterministic logic and browser-harness proof
for feel.

## Act I — A Machine Worth Living On *(chill foundation)*

The calm phases get a purpose beyond building walls.

### Phase 1 — Machine damage & repair *(in flight)*

Per-piece health, destructible walls, subsystem damage, repair tool.
Completes the damage half of the defend→repair loop before anything shoots at
the machine in earnest.

### Phase 2 — The opening & title screen

The first thing a player sees, built now as a placeholder to be expanded
later:

- **Title screen:** code-generated like everything else — the machine walking
  the dunes as a live background (the fixed free camera already exists), logo,
  New Game / Continue / Settings. Replaces booting straight into gameplay.
- **Opening scene:** a scripted rooftop sequence. The player spawns on a ruined
  building rooftop; scavenger robots chase them across it; they are backed
  into a corner on a ledge; the machine walks past below; they jump onto the
  deck, and the machine carries them forward into the endless desert. Minimal
  scripting, skippable, explicitly a placeholder for a fuller authored opening
  later.

This phase gives the game a front door and a story hook in the first sixty
seconds. Full menu polish (settings depth, onboarding) stays in Phase 15.

### Phase 3 — Power & light

The generator burns the fuel that is currently inert. Power capacity, device
draw, simple priority groups. Lamps — which fixes the dark-interiors gap.
The refinery and all future turrets become powered devices. Fuel appears in
the salvage field, so keeping the lights on becomes a gentle standing
pressure.

### Phase 4 — Home & hearth *(light survival)*

Water and food as forgiving needs: a condenser, a stove, a small planter box.
Decoration pieces (chairs, rugs, shelves — cheap to build, no gameplay
weight). Ambient interior sound and the machine's first music. Running dry
slows sprint or healing; it never kills.

**Act check:** a calm stretch is genuinely pleasant and purposeful — reel in
fuel and water, cook, keep the lights burning, decorate, repair.

---

## Act II — A Machine Worth Defending *(combat rise)*

The machine stops being scenery in its own fights.

### Phase 5 — Hardpoints & turrets

Light hardpoints on the build grid, one manual turret, one automated turret,
both drawing power (which is why this follows Phase 3). Target filtering for
the automated turret.

### Phase 6 — Enemy vehicles

The first crewed enemy: a skiff with visible human raiders aboard. Intercept
choreography (spawn ahead, close, hold a combat lane alongside), hostile fire
that uses Phase 1's localized damage, destroyable, drops loot. Telegraphed by
the threat director — dust plume, engine note — before it arrives.

### Phase 7 — Boarding *(the signature encounter)*

A boarding vehicle pulls alongside, matches speed, fires a hook. Boarders
cross onto the deck and fight through the structure the player built —
corridors, doorways, chokepoints. Counterplay at every stage: shoot crew
early, destroy the vehicle, cut the hook, hold a doorway.
`BOARDING HOOK ATTACHED — LEFT DECK`.

**Act check:** the handoff's §60 success moment works end to end — hear it
coming, fight from the roof, fail to stop the hook, hold the doorway, survive,
repair.

---

## Act III — A Story Worth Following *(loot, radio, first steering)*

### Phase 8 — Loot & progression

Rarity tiers, weapon drops, behavior-changing weapon mods, and
machine-component drops (better turrets, engines, generators) so a good
machine part is as exciting as a good gun. A minimal unlock system
(`src/progression/` is waiting for it) that later phases hang story and
navigation unlocks on.

### Phase 9 — The radio & Chapter 1

The radio device, the signal, and the first hand-authored story destination —
directly on the path, since the player cannot steer yet (a dead mega-walker
half-buried in the dunes is the natural candidate). Journals, environmental
storytelling, a unique reward, the lead to the next signal. This phase also
fixes the story premise for real, including how the rooftop opening connects
to it.

### Phase 10 — Navigation, tier 1

The reward from Chapter 1: limited course adjustment — a few degrees, at fuel
cost, on cooldown. Points of interest start appearing off-path (wreck fields,
rich salvage, minor landmarks), and some now pass out of reach *by design*.
The frustration is the point; it sells the later tiers.

**Act check:** the player follows a signal to a place with a story in it, and
steering toward a distant wreck feels like a meaningful spend.

---

## Act IV — A World Worth Crossing *(variety, escalation, story middle)*

### Phase 11 — The enemy fleet

Two or three more vehicle classes with distinct jobs (a light crawler that
flanks and shoots, a salvager that steals from exposed storage, a sniper rig
that punishes exposed structure). The threat director learns to compose mixed
encounters and scale to the machine's power.

### Phase 12 — The changing desert

Terrain regions with mechanical teeth: deep sand slows, rock fields threaten
damage, ruins bring cover and loot, storms compress visibility and mask
attacks. The route itself becomes a character.

### Phase 13 — Chapters 2 & 3, navigation tier 2

Two more destinations — now off-path, requiring the steering the story
granted, larger and more authored than Chapter 1 (answering: what is the
machine, why the route, what is at the end). Navigation tier 2 (15–30° course
changes) unlocks mid-act so Chapter 3's destination is a real navigation
exercise.

**Act check:** two sessions in different stretches of desert feel different —
different ground, different enemies — and the story is pulling toward
somewhere specific.

---

## Act V — Arrival *(ending and release)*

### Phase 14 — The final destination

The last signal, a climactic arrival encounter that uses everything (defense
under fire while the machine crosses the worst terrain in the game), the
ending, and a post-game "keep driving" mode so the sandbox survives the
credits.

### Phase 15 — Release polish

Full menu flow building on Phase 2's title screen, in-world onboarding for the
first 20 minutes (replacing debug-key knowledge), settings and key rebinding,
accessibility basics, a music pass, performance hardening, save-format
lockdown, itch.io-shaped deploy.

**1.0 definition of done:** a new player with no instructions can start, learn
by playing, follow the story to the end in roughly 8–15 hours, and keep
playing after.

---

## 5. Explicitly post-1.0 (the expansion shelf)

Player boarding and capturing enemy machines, drones and automation,
blueprints, co-op, new biomes and story chapters, roaming-fortress raid
bosses, machine-to-machine grappling.

## 6. Standing constraints (unchanged)

- Procedural art by default; CC0 textures only where they earn their place,
  governed by `ASSETS.md`.
- The machine never moves; the world scrolls past it.
- Fixed 60Hz simulation, typed event bus, stats in `src/data/`.
- Every phase ships playable, with unit tests for deterministic logic and
  browser-harness proof for feel.
- The stairs scripted-proof gap gets closed inside Phase 1 or Phase 7,
  whichever touches enemy pathing over stairs first.

## 7. How to use this document

One phase at a time: brainstorm the phase → write its design spec in
`docs/superpowers/specs/` → write its implementation plan in
`docs/superpowers/plans/` → implement → the act check at each act boundary is
a real play session, not a checklist. Reorder within an act freely if
implementation teaches something; reordering across acts means revisiting this
document.
